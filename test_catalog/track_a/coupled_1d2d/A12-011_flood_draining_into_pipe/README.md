# A12-011 — Flood draining into a pipe

## Physics

A walled 30 × 30 m yard with a flat floor holds 0.3 m of water (270 m³). A
gully at its centre, whose crest stands 0.05 m above the floor, drains into
manhole M1 and a 0.3 m, 100 m pipe falling 1 m to a free outfall.

* **Final level**: the water above the crest drains; the 5 cm below it
  cannot. The yard settles at the crest level, 0.05 m.
* **Discharged volume**: \(270 - 900 \times 0.05 = 225\) m³ leaves by the
  outfall (the pipe drains freely and holds nothing at the end).

At first the gully could take far more than the pipe carries, so the manhole
surcharges and the inflow is set by the pipe; at the end the last centimetres
drain slowly over the crest (a weir slows as the head to the 3/2), so after
two hours the level is a few millimetres above the crest for any reasonable
exchange law.

## What it checks

Surface-to-network exchange limited by a surcharged pipe, the crest cut-off,
and the coupled water balance.
